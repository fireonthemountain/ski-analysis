# Skiing Technique Reference Guide

This document serves as a reference for the skiing techniques and metrics analyzed by the Ski Analysis application.

## Overview

Good skiing technique involves coordination of multiple body parts and proper weight distribution. This guide covers the key elements that our analysis system evaluates.

## 1. Stance and Balance

### Athletic Stance
- **Neutral Position**: Body balanced over the center of skis
- **Forward Lean**: Slight forward lean from ankles (not waist)
- **Flexion**: Knees and ankles flexed, ready to absorb terrain
- **Hip-Ankle Alignment**: Hips stacked over ankles

### Center of Mass (COM)
- COM should be centered between feet laterally
- COM should be slightly forward of boot center
- Dynamic adjustment based on terrain and turn phase

### Common Issues
- ❌ Sitting back (weight too far back)
- ❌ Locked knees (insufficient flexion)
- ❌ Leaning from waist instead of ankles

## 2. Edge Control

### Edge Angle
- **Definition**: Angle between ski base and snow surface
- **Carving**: Higher edge angles (30°-60°+)
- **Skidding**: Lower edge angles (10°-30°)
- **Flat Ski**: 0° edge angle

### Pressure Distribution
- **Inside Ski**: Lighter pressure, guidance
- **Outside Ski**: Primary pressure, ~70-80% weight
- **Transition**: Brief moment of equal pressure

### Measurements
- Edge angle (degrees)
- Pressure ratio (inside:outside)
- Edge engagement timing

## 3. Turn Dynamics

### Turn Phases

**1. Initiation**
- Release of old edges
- Weight transfer begins
- Upper body moves toward new turn

**2. Control Phase**
- Edge angle increases
- Maximum pressure on outside ski
- Arc of turn established

**3. Completion**
- Speed control
- Preparation for next turn
- Smooth transition

### Turn Shape
- **Short Radius**: Quick, tight turns (moguls, steep terrain)
- **Medium Radius**: All-mountain turns
- **Long Radius**: High-speed, GS-style turns

### Metrics
- Turn radius (meters)
- Turn duration (seconds)
- Rhythm consistency
- Transition time between turns

## 4. Upper Body Position

### Alignment
- **Shoulders**: Parallel to fall line or slightly downhill
- **Arms**: Forward and wide, pole ready
- **Head**: Looking ahead, not down at tips
- **Torso**: Stable, minimal rotation

### Counter and Angulation
- **Counter**: Upper body facing down hill while skis turn
- **Angulation**: Hip angles toward inside of turn
- **Separation**: Independent upper/lower body movement

### Common Issues
- ❌ Rotation (spinning shoulders with turn)
- ❌ Arms trailing behind
- ❌ Looking at ski tips

## 5. Lower Body Movement

### Knee Angles
- **Flexion**: 20-40° depending on phase
- **Inside Knee**: More flexed in turn
- **Outside Knee**: Strong, driving through turn

### Hip Movement
- **Angulation**: Hip moves inside turn while shoulders stay level
- **Extension/Flexion**: Dynamic range for terrain absorption
- **Forward Position**: Hips ahead of heels

### Ankle Flex
- **Shin Pressure**: Constant contact with boot tongue
- **Flex Angle**: 15-25° forward lean in boots
- **Active Flexion**: Ankles work to maintain position

## 6. Pole Plant

### Timing
- Plant initiates turn
- Planted as skis cross fall line
- Brief touch, not a support

### Position
- **Forward**: Pole planted ahead of boot
- **Downhill**: Planted on downhill/outside of turn
- **Wrist**: Active wrist motion, not arm swing

## Analysis Metrics

### Quantifiable Metrics

| Metric | Ideal Range | Common Issues |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Forward Lean | 15-25° | <10° (sitting back), >30° (too far forward) |
| Edge Angle | 30-60° | <20° (skidding), >70° (over-edged) |
| Knee Flexion | 20-40° | <15° (too straight), >50° (over-flexed) |
| Turn Rhythm | Consistent | Irregular timing, rushed transitions |
| Upper/Lower Separation | 20-40° | <10° (rotation), >50° (over-twisted) |
| Weight Distribution | 70:30 outside:inside | 50:50 (backseat), 90:10 (too aggressive) |

### Qualitative Assessments

- **Flow**: Smooth, rhythmic movements vs. jerky, abrupt
- **Adaptability**: Terrain response and adjustment
- **Style**: Technique appropriate for conditions and ability
- **Efficiency**: Minimal wasted energy

## Terrain Considerations

### Groomed Runs
- Focus on clean carving
- Higher edge angles
- Consistent rhythm

### Moguls
- Absorption and extension
- Quick turn initiation
- Upper body stability

### Powder
- More equal weight distribution
- Bouncing motion
- Wider stance

### Ice/Hardpack
- Precise edge control
- Strong angulation
- Patient turn completion

## Skill Progression

### Beginner Focus
- Basic stance and balance
- Smooth weight transfer
- Wedge to parallel transition

### Intermediate Focus
- Edge control and carving
- Turn shape variety
- Speed management

### Advanced Focus
- Dynamic turn radius adjustment
- Terrain adaptation
- Racing line optimization

### Expert Focus
- Subtle edge adjustments
- Perfect timing and rhythm
- Style refinement

## Video Analysis Tips

### Camera Angles

**Front View (Optimal)**
- See edge angles clearly
- Observe upper body rotation
- Check arm position

**Side View**
- Evaluate forward/back balance
- See knee/ankle flexion
- Check hip position

**Behind/Follow Cam**
- See turn shape
- Observe rhythm
- Check line choice

### Recording Tips
- Stable camera (tripod or gimbal)
- Full body in frame
- Multiple consecutive turns
- Good lighting
- Clean background
